Our History & Culture

Stories, traditions, and festivals of Central Visayas.

Brief History

Hover over a year to read its history.

Before 1500s

Cebu, Bohol, Negros Oriental, and Siquijor had established communities engaged in farming, fishing, trading, and cultural exchange.

10th to 14th centuries

Cebu, then known as Sugbo, developed as an important trading center with Asian merchants.

1521

Ferdinand Magellan arrived in Cebu. Rajah Humabon welcomed the expedition, and Christianity was introduced.

1521

The Battle of Mactan took place. Lapu-Lapu and his warriors defeated Magellan's forces.

1565

Miguel López de Legazpi established the first permanent Spanish settlement in Cebu, beginning Spanish colonization.

1595

The Diocese of Cebu was established, strengthening the spread of Christianity.

1898

The Philippine Revolution against Spanish rule reached its final stages, and Spanish colonial rule ended.

1899 to 1901

American colonial administration expanded throughout the Visayas.

1937

Cebu City officially became a chartered city on February 24.

1941 to 1945

Japanese forces occupied parts of Central Visayas during World War II.

1945

Allied forces liberated Cebu and other areas from Japanese occupation.

1972

Presidential Decree No. 1 reorganized the country's regional administrative structure, establishing the framework for the regions.

2024

Republic Act No. 12000 established the Negros Island Region, transferring Negros Oriental and Siquijor out of Central Visayas.

Cultural Highlights

Festivals

Sinulog Festival

January (Third Sunday), Cebu City, Cebu

Sandugo Festival

July, Tagbilaran City, Bohol

Buglasan Festival

October, Dumaguete City, Negros Oriental

Traditional Crafts and Foods

Raffia Weaving

A traditional craft in Bohol that uses natural fibers to create bags, mats, accessories, and other decorative products.

Dried Mangoes

Sweet and chewy mango slices that are dried and preserved, making them one of Cebu's most popular food products.

Cebu Guitars

Handmade musical instruments crafted by skilled Cebuano artisans, particularly in Lapu-Lapu City. They are known for their quality and craftsmanship.
